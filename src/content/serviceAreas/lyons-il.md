## Restoration emergencies common in Lyons

Lyons sits along the Des Plaines River, and that geography drives a lot of what we see here. Heavy spring rain and fast snowmelt push the river toward its banks, and homes on lower-lying streets near the river corridor take on water through basement walls, window wells, and floor drains that back up under pressure. Combine that with an older housing stock built on shallow foundations and you get a pattern of recurring basement moisture that's as much about site drainage as it is about a single storm event. Winter brings its own version of the same problem: frozen supply lines and ice-damming on older roof assemblies that let meltwater track down into attics and exterior walls before anyone notices a stain.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Lyons

Much of Lyons was built up in the early to mid-20th century as a close-in Chicago suburb, which means brick bungalows and frame homes with full basements, galvanized or early copper supply lines, and cast iron drain stacks that are now well past their original service life. Basements here are a liability during river-stage rises and heavy rain events, since shallow footings and older foundation parging don't always keep pace with hydrostatic pressure.
