## Restoration emergencies common in Evergreen Park

Evergreen Park sits in a part of Cook County where heavy clay soil and a flat water table combine with aging combined sewer infrastructure, a pattern that shows up every summer when intense thunderstorms overwhelm storm drains faster than they can clear. Basements built decades ago, many without modern sump systems or with undersized ones, take on water through floor drains and foundation cracks. Winters bring a different risk: deep frost lines crack older clay and cast-iron drain lines, and frozen or burst supply pipes inside uninsulated crawlspaces and attached garages are a recurring call every cold snap. Add in the area's mix of older housing stock with original wiring and aging HVAC, and fire and smoke damage follow a similarly predictable seasonal curve.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Evergreen Park

Much of Evergreen Park's housing dates to the post-war building boom of the 1940s through 1960s, brick bungalows and ranches built on full basements, a layout that means water intrusion almost always means a basement problem first. Plumbing from that era is a mix of galvanized supply lines and cast-iron drains, both of which corrode and crack with age, and older homes may still carry asbestos-containing materials in floor tile, pipe wrap, or plaster that a restoration scope has to account for before demolition begins.

The heavy clay soil common across this part of Cook County holds water against foundations longer than sandier soil would, which slows drainage after storms and puts added hydrostatic pressure on basement walls.
