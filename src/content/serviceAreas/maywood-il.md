## Restoration emergencies common in Maywood

Maywood sits close to the Des Plaines River, and the surrounding area has a documented history of river and overland flooding during heavy spring rain and fast snowmelt. Combine that with an aging combined or partially-separated sewer system typical of older Cook County suburbs, and basement backups become a recurring call type here, not an occasional one. Add Chicago-area winters with hard freeze-thaw cycles, and you get a second pattern: pipes that split in unheated crawlspaces or exterior walls, then thaw and flood a finished basement before anyone's home to catch it. Housing stock that predates modern vapor barriers and sump systems means water that gets into a Maywood basement often has nowhere fast to go.

## Services we provide in Maywood

**Water damage restoration** in Maywood frequently starts at the lowest point in the house, a basement rec room or mechanical space where water has nowhere to drain once it's in. We extract standing water, pull wet baseboard and drywall where needed, and set up monitored drying rather than guessing when a space is safe to close back up.

**Fire and smoke damage cleanup** has to account for the older plaster-and-lath or early-drywall construction common in Maywood's housing stock, which holds smoke odor differently than newer assemblies. We clean and seal affected surfaces rather than just masking the smell.

**Mold remediation** usually follows a water event that sat too long, especially in basements with limited ventilation. Mold can begin colonizing in as little as 24 to 48 hours on wet drywall or framing, so timing between the water event and the drying response matters more than most homeowners expect.

**Storm and flood damage response** covers both river-adjacent flooding near the Des Plaines and the more common scenario of a sewer backup or window well failure during a heavy rain event.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Maywood

Much of Maywood's housing dates to the early-to-mid 20th century, with wood-frame construction, plaster walls in older sections, and a mix of full basements and crawlspaces beneath. Plumbing from that era commonly includes galvanized supply lines and cast iron drains, both of which corrode from the inside out over decades and tend to fail at joints, usually discovered only after a slow leak has already soaked subfloor or framing. Homes built before the 1980s may also contain asbestos-containing materials in older flooring, pipe insulation, or plaster, which changes how a restoration crew scopes demolition.

The village sits on relatively flat ground near the Des Plaines River floodplain, which affects drainage during heavy rain and is a factor insurers and contractors both account for when scoping basement work.
