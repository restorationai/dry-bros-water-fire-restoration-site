## Restoration emergencies common in Stickney

Stickney sits low relative to the Des Plaines River, and its Cook County storm sewers were not built for the rainfall volumes the region now sees several times a year. When sewers surcharge, water looks for the path of least resistance, which is often the floor drain or the oldest section of clay or cast iron drain tile running under a 1940s or 1950s basement slab. Add a long Chicago-area winter with multiple freeze-thaw cycles, and supply lines that run along uninsulated exterior walls or through unheated crawlspaces are at real risk of splitting. Between sewer backup, groundwater intrusion, and freeze-related pipe failures, basements are where most Stickney restoration calls originate.

## Services we provide in Stickney

Water damage restoration is our most frequent call in Stickney, and it usually involves a finished or partially finished basement where water has already wicked into drywall, baseboards, and carpet pad before anyone notices standing water. We extract, set containment, and monitor drying with moisture meters rather than guessing based on how a surface feels.

Sewage cleanup is closely related here given how often municipal and combined sewer backups show up through floor drains and basement fixtures during heavy storm events. This work requires more than mopping: affected porous materials typically need to come out, and the area is disinfected and dried under containment.

Fire and smoke damage cleanup addresses both the structure and the secondary damage soot leaves behind, since smoke residue is acidic and keeps etching metal and finishes until it is professionally removed. Mold remediation follows naturally from any water event that sat for more than a day or two, since mold can begin colonizing damp drywall and framing within 24 to 48 hours. We also handle storm damage and emergency board-up after wind or hail compromises a roof or window, stabilizing the structure before the next weather system moves through.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Stickney

Much of Stickney's housing dates to the postwar boom of the 1940s through 1960s: modest brick bungalows and ranch homes on full or partial basements, often with cast iron drain lines and galvanized or early copper supply piping that is now well past typical service life. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out and tends to fail at joints, while aging cast iron drain lines are prone to cracking and root intrusion. Homes from this era may also contain asbestos in old floor tile, pipe wrap, or plaster, which a restoration scope has to account for before demolition begins.

The village sits near the Des Plaines River and low-lying drainage areas, so basements can see groundwater pressure from below in addition to surface water from above during heavy rain. Structural repairs and rebuild work generally require a permit through the Village of Stickney's building department, with Cook County and Illinois state plumbing and electrical code governing the trade work itself; a licensed contractor should always confirm current permit triggers before opening walls or replacing systems.
