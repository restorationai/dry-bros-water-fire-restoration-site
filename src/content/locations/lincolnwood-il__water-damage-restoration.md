## Why Lincolnwood Properties See Water Damage Issues

Lincolnwood's housing stock is mostly post-World War II construction: brick ranches and split-levels built on slab or shallow basement foundations through the 1950s and 60s. Cook County's heavy clay soil doesn't drain fast, so after a hard rain or quick snowmelt, water tends to sit against foundation walls instead of moving away from the house. Combined with aging clay or cast-iron sewer laterals common to homes of this era, that pressure is a frequent cause of basement backups we see across the north suburbs. Flat grading throughout the village also means downspouts and window wells that aren't maintained can send water straight toward the foundation rather than away from it, which is a problem we check for on nearly every call.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Lincolnwood

The first step is always extraction, pulling standing water off flooring and out of carpet pad before it migrates further into subfloor and framing. In homes with finished basements built on slab, we check for moisture trapped between the slab and any vapor barrier or subfloor product installed later, since that layer can hold water long after the surface looks dry. We set drying equipment calibrated to the room's size and the materials involved, then monitor moisture content in wood framing, drywall, and subfloor daily until readings return to a normal range. Where original plaster or lath is still present in older sections of a home, drying takes longer than drywall because the material releases moisture more slowly, so we adjust our timeline and equipment placement accordingly rather than pulling drying mats early.
