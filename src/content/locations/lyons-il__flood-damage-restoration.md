Cook County's clay-heavy soil doesn't drain the way sandier soil does further west, so once the Des Plaines River or a storm surge overwhelms local drainage, water tends to sit rather than percolate away. Those basements often have masonry block foundations with hairline cracks that let groundwater seep in slowly during a flood event, which means a homeowner can come home to several inches of water with no obvious single point of entry. ## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Lyons

We start with extraction, pulling standing water out with truck-mounted pumps before it has more time to wick into drywall, baseboards, and subfloor. From there we inspect for contamination level, floodwater from a river overflow or sewer backup is treated differently than clean water from a supply line, since it may carry silt, bacteria, or chemical runoff that changes how materials get handled. Once the space is cleared, we set drying equipment calibrated to the room's moisture load and monitor with moisture meters until the structure reads dry, not just dry to the touch.

## Local Note

A detail that catches a lot of Lyons homeowners off guard: when the Des Plaines River rises enough to affect a block, the groundwater table underneath nearby homes rises with it, even ones that aren't visibly flooded on the surface. That means a basement can take on water through the floor slab or foundation joints days after the river has already crested and the street looks dry again.
