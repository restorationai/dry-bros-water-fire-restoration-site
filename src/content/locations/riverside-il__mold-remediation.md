## Why Riverside Properties See Mold Issues

Riverside's position along the Des Plaines River floodplain means basements and crawlspaces in many homes sit closer to the water table than most Chicago suburbs. Combine that with a housing stock that includes a significant number of homes built in the late 1800s and early 1900s as part of the village's original Olmsted-designed layout, and you get older foundations, original masonry, and plumbing that wasn't built with modern vapor barriers in mind. Spring thaw and heavy summer storms push groundwater up against foundation walls, and if a sump pump lags or a window well fills, moisture finds its way into framing and subfloor within days. Mold doesn't wait for a major flood to take hold; a slow, unnoticed leak behind a baseboard or around an old cast iron drain line is often enough.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Riverside

We start with a moisture inspection using thermal imaging and a moisture meter to map how far contamination has traveled past the visible stain, which matters more in Riverside's older homes where plaster and lath hide moisture longer than drywall does. Once we know the scope, we set up physical containment with negative air pressure to keep spores from migrating into living spaces while we work, especially important in homes with forced-air systems original to an older build. Affected porous materials that can't be cleaned are removed and bagged for disposal, while structural wood and masonry are treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial and dried per the IICRC S500 standard. We finish by running air scrubbers and verifying moisture content before any rebuild work begins.

## Local note

Homes close to the Des Plaines River in Riverside frequently have fieldstone or rubble foundations rather than poured concrete, a detail from the original 19th-century construction. These foundations breathe and wick moisture differently than modern basements, which means a musty smell in a Riverside basement can mean active moisture movement through the stone itself, not just a surface leak. We test the foundation material directly rather than relying only on surface readings, because treating the symptom without addressing what the stone is absorbing leads to recurring mold within a season.
