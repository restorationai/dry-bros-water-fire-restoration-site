Riverside's tree-lined streets and century-old frame houses mean a kitchen fire or electrical fire rarely stays contained to one room the way it might in a newer subdivision, so the first hours after the flames are out matter as much as the fire itself.

## Why Riverside Properties See Fire Damage Spread Faster

Much of Riverside was built in the late 1800s and early 1900s as part of the village's original Olmsted-designed plan, and that era of construction leaned heavily on balloon framing. Unlike modern platform framing, balloon-framed walls run as open channels from the basement sill straight up into the attic. A fire that starts in a wall outlet or behind a baseboard heater can travel through that cavity and show up in the attic before anyone upstairs smells smoke. Detached garages and carriage houses, common on Riverside's deep historic lots, add another wrinkle: a garage fire can scorch siding and melt soffits on the main house well before it ever reaches the interior, which means smoke and heat damage assessments often need to cover two structures instead of one. Mature tree canopy throughout the village also traps smoke odor against siding and window screens longer than it would in a more open neighborhood, so exterior cleaning is part of the job more often here than elsewhere.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Riverside

We start with a walkthrough to separate what's structurally sound from what isn't, because in a balloon-framed house that assessment has to include wall cavities and attic framing, not just visible char. Board-up and tarping come first if the fire department opened a roof or broke windows for ventilation. From there we run air scrubbers and begin soot and soda-blasting on salvageable framing, since raw wood that's been exposed to smoke for even a day starts absorbing odor into the grain. Contents go through a pack-out, ultrasonic cleaning where appropriate, and ozone or hydroxyl treatment depending on how deep the smoke penetrated fabric and upholstery. Demolition of damaged plaster and lath, which is still common in Riverside's older homes, is done carefully since plaster keying and lath patterns sometimes need to match for historic repair work later. We document every step with photos for the adjuster and for any village permitting review before reconstruction starts.

## Riverside Insurance & Historic District Coordination

Because Riverside is a National Historic Landmark village, exterior repairs, especially on street-facing elevations, often go through review by the village's historic preservation process before a contractor can rebuild siding, trim, or roofing to match. That review can add time to a claim timeline that an adjuster in a non-historic suburb wouldn't expect, so we flag it early and build it into the scope of work we submit. We photograph and measure original materials, like clapboard profiles and window trim, before demolition so replacement pieces can be matched rather than guessed at. That documentation also tends to speed up the historic review itself, since the commission is looking at exactly what was there before the fire.

## Local note

One thing that catches Riverside homeowners off guard: because so many lots back up to the Des Plaines River and its floodplain, fire department access on some streets is narrower than a standard suburban grid, with curving lanes and limited turnaround space that Frederick Law Olmsted designed intentionally in the 1870s. That same layout can slow staging of drying equipment and dumpsters after a fire, so we often scout truck access and parking before equipment arrives rather than assuming a straight pull-up to the curb.
