That proximity matters after a fire: smoke and soot travel through shared attic spaces, siding gaps, and older HVAC runs faster than most homeowners expect, and a fire that starts in one unit can leave a visible residue film two or three doors down. Older wiring and knob-and-tube remnants still found in some of these homes are also a common ignition point, which means the fire marshal's report often points investigators (and us) toward the electrical panel or an outdated junction box as a starting point for the loss.

## Why Maywood Properties See Fire Damage Issues

A mix of aging electrical systems, close-set lot lines, and older gas furnaces contributes to the fire patterns we see across this part of Cook County. Homes near the Des Plaines River floodplain also carry a secondary risk: a fire suppression response can leave standing water in basements that were already prone to seasonal dampness, which turns a fire job into a combined fire-and-water mitigation project within days if it's not addressed quickly. We treat that combination as the rule rather than the exception out here, not an afterthought.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Maywood

We start with a structural safety walkthrough, because older joists and lath-and-plaster ceilings can hide heat damage that isn't visible from below. From there, the sequence is consistent: board-up and tarping to secure the structure, water extraction from firefighting runoff, soot and smoke residue removal from framing and remaining contents, and air scrubbing to pull particulate and odor out of the structure before any rebuild begins. On brick two-flats and bungalows with shared walls, we pay close attention to adjoining units and party walls, since soot migration through old masonry joints is common and often gets missed by crews who aren't used to this building type.

One thing that trips up out-of-area crews working in Maywood: a lot of the two-flats and bungalows here were converted or added onto over the decades, which means ductwork and electrical runs don't always match what's on the original permit drawings.
