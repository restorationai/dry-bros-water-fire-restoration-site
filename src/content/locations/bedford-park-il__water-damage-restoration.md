## Why Bedford Park Properties See Water Damage Issues

Most of the buildings we're called to here aren't single-family homes, they're warehouses, distribution facilities, and light-industrial properties with flat or low-slope roofing systems. Flat roofs pond water during heavy Midwest storms, and once a membrane seam or drain fails, the water finds its way down through insulation and ceiling tile into office space, inventory, or mechanical rooms below. Cook County's older combined sewer infrastructure also means that intense rainfall can back up floor drains in ground-level industrial spaces faster than a standard sump pump can keep pace. Add in the freeze-thaw cycles Chicago-area buildings go through every winter, and exposed or poorly insulated pipe runs in unheated warehouse sections become a recurring source of pipe bursts once temperatures swing.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Bedford Park

We start by identifying the source and shutting it down, whether that's a shut-off valve on a burst line or a temporary patch on a roof drain. From there we extract standing water with truck-mounted or portable extraction units depending on how much square footage we're dealing with, since a 2,000-square-foot warehouse bay moves differently than a residential basement. Moisture mapping comes next: we check subfloor and slab moisture content, wall cavities, and any drop ceiling systems, because concrete slabs in industrial buildings hold and release moisture on a different timeline than wood subfloor. Structural drying is calibrated to that, with air movers and dehumidification positioned to dry the space per IICRC S500 drying standards rather than guessing at a one-size-fits-all setup. We document moisture readings throughout so the drying log holds up if an insurance adjuster or property manager asks for proof the space was actually dry before we closed the job out.
