## Why Evergreen Park Properties See Water Damage Issues

Much of Evergreen Park's housing stock dates to the postwar boom, brick bungalows and Cape Cods built on poured concrete or block foundations, many with finished or partially finished basements added decades after original construction. That later basement work often means moisture barriers and drainage systems that weren't part of the original build. Combine that with Cook County's dense clay till, which holds water against foundation walls instead of letting it percolate down, and you get hydrostatic pressure that pushes moisture through hairline cracks long after a storm has passed. Add an older sanitary or combined sewer line serving the block, and a hard rainfall can send water back up through floor drains before it ever reaches the street.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Evergreen Park

We start every call with moisture mapping, because a basement that looks dry on the surface can still be saturated behind drywall or under a finished floor. In bungalow basements we're frequently pulling baseboards and drilling small weep holes in block walls to relieve trapped water before it migrates further. Extraction comes first, using truck-mounted or portable pumps depending on how much water is standing and how accessible the area is. From there we set air movers and dehumidifiers calibrated to the room's size and the materials involved, concrete block holds and releases moisture more slowly than drywall, so a block foundation basement often needs several extra days of monitored drying before we're comfortable calling it dry. We track moisture content daily and document readings for the insurance file, following the IICRC S500 water damage standard as a baseline for drying targets.

One thing only comes up once you've worked a few basements out here: a lot of Evergreen Park's older blocks share lateral sewer connections that tie multiple homes into a single line before it reaches the main.
