## Why Brookfield Properties See Flood Damage Issues

Brookfield sits low relative to Salt Creek, and the creek's floodplain runs close to residential blocks on the village's east and south sides. When heavy rain hits the Chicago area, Salt Creek and the surrounding combined sewer system can back up faster than they drain, pushing water up through floor drains and foundation cracks instead of away from the house. A lot of Brookfield's housing stock, bungalows and Cape Cods built from the 1920s through the 1950s, was constructed with shallow foundations and clay tile drainage that was never designed for the volume of stormwater these systems see today. Finished basements, which are common in this part of Cook County, take the worst of it because carpet, drywall, and stored belongings sit right at the level where the water comes in first.

## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Brookfield

We start with extraction, pulling standing water out with truck-mounted pumps before it has time to wick further into drywall and subfloor. From there we assess what's salvageable: older plaster and lath in pre-1950s Brookfield homes behaves differently than modern drywall, it absorbs slower but holds onto moisture longer, so drying timelines get adjusted accordingly instead of pulled from a generic chart. Drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with air movers and dehumidifiers placed based on moisture readings we take room by room, not a flat equipment count. Once materials read dry, we document everything with photos and moisture logs for your insurance file and rebuild whatever had to come out, from baseboards to subfloor.

## Local note

Homes closer to Salt Creek, particularly along the eastern edge of the village, often have sump pumps and French drains added decades after the original build, retrofits rather than original equipment. We check backup power on these systems as part of our initial walkthrough, because a pump that isn't running is the same as no pump at all.
