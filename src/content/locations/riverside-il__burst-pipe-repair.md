**Burst pipe in Riverside?** Water from a split supply line can move through a historic home's lath and plaster faster than most homeowners expect, and in a village built on the Des Plaines River floodplain, that water often has nowhere easy to drain. Dry Bros answers 24/7 and gets a crew moving on burst pipe cleanup and repair the moment you call, before a cracked line turns into a collapsed ceiling or a mold problem in the crawlspace below.

## Why Riverside Properties See Burst Pipe Problems

Riverside's housing stock is older than most of the western suburbs, with many homes dating to the village's original 1870s Olmsted and Vaux plan and a good share of early 1900s construction filling in the curvilinear streets since. That means galvanized steel and early copper supply lines are still common, and both corrode and fatigue from the inside long before a homeowner sees a visible leak. Add in the clay and shallow fieldstone foundations typical of the era, and a hard freeze along the river bottom can push frost deep enough to split a line running through an uninsulated crawlspace or exterior wall. The floodplain soil itself stays saturated longer after storms, which keeps buried water lines under more pressure and stress than they'd see on higher, better-drained ground.

## Our Burst Pipe Cleanup and Repair Process in Riverside

The first step is always stopping the source, so we confirm the main shutoff location with you (older Riverside homes sometimes have shutoffs in unusual spots, tucked behind root-cellar style crawlspace access or buried under decades of basement storage). From there we extract standing water, then map moisture into the surrounding plaster, subfloor, and joists with meters rather than guesswork, since plaster walls common to the village's historic homes hold water deep in the keyway even when the surface feels dry. Pipe repair itself depends on what we find: a clean point repair on a single corroded section, or a conversation with you about re-piping a run that's clearly failing elsewhere too. We dry the structure to the IICRC S500 standard, checking moisture content daily rather than pulling equipment on a fixed schedule, which matters more in plaster-and-lath construction than in a typical drywall remodel.

## Local note

A detail specific to Riverside: many of the village's historic homes have shallow, unheated crawlspaces built for an era before modern insulation codes, and supply lines routed through those spaces are far more exposed to freeze-thaw cycling off the river bottom than a comparable pipe in a full basement. When we find a burst pipe in one of these crawlspaces, we check the rest of the run in that space for stress fractures and soft spots, since a single frozen section usually means the whole exposed line is on borrowed time.
