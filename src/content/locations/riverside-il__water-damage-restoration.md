## Why Riverside Properties See Water Damage Issues

Riverside sits low relative to the Des Plaines River, and that geography shapes how water moves through the village. Heavy spring rain or a fast snowmelt upstream can push river levels high enough to back up storm drains and saturate soil around foundations long before the river itself crests. Add to that a housing stock heavy with homes built in the late 1800s and early 1900s, many with original brick or rubble foundations, and you get basements that were never built to modern waterproofing standards. Older cast iron and galvanized plumbing common in these homes also tends to fail from the inside out, with corrosion narrowing pipes for years before a joint finally gives way. Mature tree canopy throughout the village, part of what makes Riverside's curvilinear streets so distinctive, also means tree roots regularly find their way into clay sewer laterals, which shows up as slow-draining fixtures or sewage backup during heavy rain.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Riverside

The first call is about stopping the source and assessing what's actually wet, not just what's visible. In a home with original plaster walls or old-growth lath, water travels differently than it does through modern drywall; it can wick upward and sideways well beyond the visible stain, which is why we check moisture readings at multiple heights and several feet past the obvious damage. Extraction comes next, pulling standing water from floors, crawlspaces, and sometimes window wells that sit below grade. Structural drying follows, calibrated to the materials involved, dense old-growth lumber and plaster hold moisture longer than today's engineered lumber and drywall, so drying timelines in these homes often run longer even after the space reads dry on the surface. Air movers and dehumidifiers are placed to work with a home's existing layout rather than against it, since many Riverside houses have smaller rooms and narrower basement stairwells than newer construction allows for.

## Riverside Insurance Coordination

Most water losses in Riverside end up involving either a homeowner's policy or, in cases tied to river overflow, a separate flood policy, since standard homeowner's coverage typically excludes rising water from a natural source. We document the source of the loss carefully for this reason, because whether water came from a pipe, a backed-up sewer line, or the river itself changes which policy actually pays. We photograph and log affected materials as we go and work directly with adjusters so the paperwork doesn't become a second project on top of the drying itself.

## Local note

Basements in Riverside's older homes often have stone or brick foundation walls pointed with historic lime mortar rather than modern Portland cement, and aggressive drying equipment placed directly against those walls can pull moisture out too fast and stress the mortar joints. We adjust airflow and equipment placement in these basements accordingly, drying the space without accelerating damage to a foundation that's already outlasted several owners.
