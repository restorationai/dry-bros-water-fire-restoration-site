## Why Maywood Properties See Water Damage Issues

Maywood sits in a low-lying stretch of west Cook County along the Des Plaines River, and a lot of the village's housing predates modern stormwater codes. Many blocks still run on combined or aging clay sewer laterals that were never sized for today's heavier rain events, which means basement backups and sump failures show up here more often than in newer suburbs. Add in brick bungalows and two-flats built from the 1920s through the 1950s, with plaster-and-lath interior walls and cast iron or galvanized supply lines well past their service life, and you get a pattern of slow leaks behind walls as much as sudden pipe bursts. Garden-level units near the river are especially exposed during spring thaw and heavy summer storms, when groundwater pressure pushes moisture up through foundation cracks rather than down a floor drain.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Maywood

We start with extraction using truck-mounted or portable pumps depending on how the water entered, then move to moisture mapping with thermal imaging and penetrating meters so we know what's wet behind the drywall, not just what's visible on the surface. In older Maywood homes, that mapping step matters more than usual: plaster holds and releases moisture far more slowly than drywall, so a wall that reads dry on the surface can still be saturated at the lath. We set air movers and dehumidifiers calibrated to that slower release rate and track drying logs against the IICRC S500 standard until moisture content in the studs and subfloor returns to a stable baseline, not just until the carpet feels dry underfoot.

## Maywood Insurance & HOA Coordination

Most Maywood water losses we see involve either a homeowner's policy covering a sudden pipe failure or a rental/condo situation where the building's association and the unit owner's policy both have a stake in the claim. We document the source, the affected materials, and the drying process with photos and moisture logs so that paperwork lines up whichever way the claim gets split. For multi-unit buildings, we coordinate scheduling with property management so common-area work like shared basement mechanical rooms or stairwells doesn't stall the unit-level drying.

## Local Note

A number of Maywood's older two-flats and bungalows have sump pumps that were added decades after the home was built, often tucked into a corner of an unfinished basement with a single battery backup or none at all. When those pumps fail during a heavy storm, the water tends to spread across the whole basement slab before anyone notices, because there's no secondary drain to slow it down. If your basement has an older pump setup, it's worth knowing where the discharge line runs and whether there's a backup power source, since that's usually the first thing we check when we arrive on a storm-related call.
