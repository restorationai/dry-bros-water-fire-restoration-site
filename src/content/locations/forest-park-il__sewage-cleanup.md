## Why Forest Park Properties See Sewage Backups

Forest Park sits in an older, densely built section of Cook County where many blocks still rely on combined sewer systems, lines that carry both stormwater and wastewater in a single pipe. During heavy rain, especially the kind of fast, high-volume thunderstorms that move through the Chicago area in summer, those combined systems can surcharge faster than they drain. When that happens, water backs up through the lowest point in a building's plumbing, which is almost always a basement floor drain or a basement bathroom. Add in aging clay sewer laterals under many of the city's older bungalows and two-flats, and you get a pattern we see repeatedly: basement backups tied to storm events rather than a single plumbing failure inside the house.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Forest Park

We start by identifying the category and extent of the contamination, then shut down or isolate the affected area to stop cross-contamination into the rest of the home. Standing sewage and visibly contaminated water are extracted first, followed by removal of porous materials that cannot be sanitized, carpet padding, baseboard, and in many cases drywall cut a foot or more above the waterline. Hard surfaces, framing, and remaining structural materials are then cleaned and treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial, and the space is dried and monitored following the IICRC S500 water damage standard until moisture readings confirm the structure is dry. In basements built before mid-century, where stone or block foundations are common throughout this part of Cook County, we pay close attention to mortar joints and sump pit areas, since those are frequent re-entry points if the underlying drainage issue isn't addressed alongside the cleanup.

## Local note

Many Forest Park basements were finished decades after the home was built, often with a floor drain installed below the sewer main's natural backflow point. That configuration means a backup upstream in the municipal system can push sewage up through that drain even when nothing inside the house has failed.
