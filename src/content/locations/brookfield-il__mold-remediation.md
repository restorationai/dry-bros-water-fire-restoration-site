## Why Brookfield Properties See Mold Issues

Brookfield sits inside the Salt Creek watershed, and that geography matters more than most homeowners realize until water backs up into a basement after a heavy spring storm. A lot of the village's housing stock, especially the bungalows and Cape Cods built in the 1920s through 1940s, was constructed with rubble stone or early poured-concrete foundations that were never meant to handle sustained moisture. Those foundations breathe differently than modern poured walls: they wick groundwater slowly and release it even more slowly, which is exactly the kind of steady, low-grade dampness mold colonies prefer. Add Chicago-area summer humidity and a finished basement with carpet or paneling over the original masonry, and you've got a recipe that plays out in a lot of these older Cook County homes.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Brookfield

We start by mapping moisture, not just visible mold, because in these older foundations the surface growth is often the smallest part of the problem. Containment goes up first: negative air pressure and physical barriers so spores don't travel into the rest of the house while we work. From there, we remove and dispose of porous materials that can't be salvaged, HEPA-vacuum and treat the surfaces that can, and run air scrubbers through the affected area. Because older rubble and block foundations hold moisture longer than modern poured concrete, we typically run dehumidification longer in Brookfield basements than we would in a newer build, and we verify dryness with moisture meters before closing anything back up.

## Brookfield Insurance & Claim Coordination

Mold claims get scrutinized differently than a straightforward pipe burst, and carriers usually want to know whether the moisture source was sudden (a failed sump pump during a storm) or gradual (a slow foundation seep nobody caught for months). We document the source, the extent of the growth, and the materials affected with photos and moisture readings, and we bill many major carriers directly so you're not fronting the full cost while the claim is reviewed. If remediation work requires removing framing, drywall, or other structural material, we coordinate with the Village of Brookfield's building department on permitting as needed, since that's handled at the municipal level rather than something we can assume case by case.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Mold

Containment barriers, negative air machines, and HEPA air scrubbers are standard on every job, but the settings and runtime change based on the structure. A finished basement with a drywall ceiling over an old stone foundation needs a different containment plan than an open crawlspace. We use moisture meters and sometimes thermal imaging to find where water is actually traveling behind walls, because mold removal that only addresses what's visible tends to come back within a season.

## Local note

A lot of Brookfield's older homes near the creek have partially below-grade basements with those original rubble stone foundations still in place, sometimes behind a layer of paneling added decades later. That paneling traps moisture against the stone and creates a hidden growth zone that doesn't show up until the smell gets noticeable upstairs. When we're working in one of these basements, we pull back enough of the finished surface to actually see the foundation before we call the job contained, because sealing mold behind a wall that's still feeding it moisture just delays the next call.
