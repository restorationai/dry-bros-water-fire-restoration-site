River Forest's name isn't decorative. The village sits close to the Des Plaines River, and that proximity shapes groundwater behavior across the whole area, not just the lots closest to the water. After a heavy spring rain or a fast snowmelt, the water table along this stretch of the river corridor rises quickly, and older foundation walls that were poured decades before modern waterproofing codes existed often show it first through a damp floor seam or a crack near the footer. Combine that with a housing stock where many homes date to the early and mid-1900s, and you get basements with fieldstone or aging poured-concrete foundations, cast iron drain lines, and sump systems that were retrofitted long after the house was built. When one of those systems is overwhelmed, even an inch of water across a slab can wick up into baseboards, insulation batting, and the bottom plates of framed walls within hours.

## Why River Forest Basements Flood

Most calls we get from this area trace back to one of three sources: a sump pump that fails or loses power during the storm that actually needs it, groundwater pressure pushing through foundation cracks after prolonged saturation, or a sewer backup when the combined storm and sanitary system downstream backs up faster than a single lateral line can clear. Village infrastructure in older Chicago-area suburbs like this one was largely built before current stormwater standards, which means heavier storms can outpace the system even when nothing on the homeowner's side has failed. Knowing which of these three is the actual source changes how we approach cleanup, because sewage-contaminated water requires different containment and disposal than clean groundwater intrusion.

## Our Basement Flooding Cleanup Process

We start with extraction, pulling standing water with truck-mounted or portable pumps depending on volume and access. From there we assess what's salvageable: drywall more than a couple inches up from the floor often has to come out regardless of whether it looks dry on the surface, because the paper facing holds moisture long after the painted side looks fine. Subfloor and framing get checked with moisture meters rather than guesswork, since a board that feels dry to the touch can still be well above the threshold where mold colonization becomes likely within 24 to 48 hours. We set air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the actual cubic footage of the space, not a generic package, and we document moisture readings throughout the dry-out so the numbers support your insurance claim rather than just our invoice.
