## Why Burbank Properties See Flood Damage Issues

Much of Burbank's housing stock went up during the postwar suburban building boom that reshaped Chicago's southwest side, meaning a large share of homes here have basements poured decades before modern waterproofing codes existed. Combine that with Cook County's older combined and partially-separated storm sewer infrastructure, and you get a pattern we see constantly: heavy summer downpours overwhelm the system faster than the ground can absorb it, and water finds the path of least resistance, usually a basement floor drain, a window well, or a crack along the footing. Flat grading across the area also means water doesn't always drain away from foundations the way it would on sloped lots, so standing water can linger against a house for hours after the rain stops.

## Our Flood Damage Restoration Process in Burbank

The first visit is about stopping the damage from spreading, not just mopping up what's visible. We extract standing water, check how far moisture has traveled into subfloor, drywall, and framing, and set containment before anything gets touched. Basements in older Burbank homes often have block or poured foundations with finished additions added later, which means moisture can travel behind a finished wall far beyond where it's visible at the surface. We pull baseboards and, where needed, drywall low enough to confirm the framing and insulation behind it are actually drying, not just reading dry at the surface while staying wet further in. Drying follows the IICRC S500 standard for structural drying, with daily moisture checks until materials hit target levels, not just until the carpet feels dry underfoot.

## Burbank Insurance Coordination

Flood losses in this area often fall into a gray zone between a standard homeowner's policy and a separate flood or sewer backup rider, and which one applies can depend on whether water came from surface flooding, groundwater, or sewer backup through a floor drain. We document the water's path, the materials affected, and moisture readings as we go, which gives your adjuster something concrete to work from instead of relying on a verbal description after the fact. If you're not sure which coverage applies, we can walk you through what we're seeing on-site so you know what to ask your carrier before the call.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Flood Damage

Flood water isn't always clean, especially when it's come up through a floor drain or mixed with groundwater runoff from the street. We classify water category on arrival and adjust our approach accordingly, from standard extraction and air movers for clear-water intrusion to more aggressive containment and antimicrobial treatment when contamination is a possibility. Submersible pumps handle standing water first, followed by truck-mounted extraction for carpet and pad, then a combination of low-grain-refrigerant dehumidifiers and axial air movers sized to the actual cubic footage of the affected space, not a one-size-fits-all setup.

## Local Note

One thing that catches homeowners off guard in this part of Cook County: a sump pump that's been working fine for years can fail the moment it's needed most, because sustained heavy rain draws far more continuous runtime than the pump ever sees during normal weather. We routinely find pumps that overheated or burned out mid-storm in homes where the owner had no idea there was a problem until water was already on the floor. If your home relies on a single sump pump with no battery backup, that's worth addressing before the next storm system rolls through, not after.
