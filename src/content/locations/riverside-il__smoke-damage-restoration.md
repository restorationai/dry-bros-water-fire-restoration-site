We answer 24/7 and start containment and odor control as soon as we're on site.

Riverside's streets were laid out along the Des Plaines River more than a century ago, and a large share of the homes that line them were built in that same era, before modern fire-stopping was standard in exterior walls. That matters for smoke jobs specifically: older balloon-frame construction runs open stud channels straight from the basement to the attic, and smoke finds that path before it finds anything else. A fire that looks contained to one room can leave soot staining an attic three stories up, and homeowners are often surprised when we find residue in spaces that never saw flame.

## Why Riverside Properties See Smoke Damage Issues

Between the river's humidity and Chicago-area winters that push furnaces and fireplaces hard for months at a time, Riverside sees a steady run of heating-related smoke incidents alongside the usual kitchen and electrical fires. Humid summer air also plays a role after the fact: smoke residue that isn't fully removed within the first few days can re-bond to porous surfaces as humidity rises, which is part of why we treat odor control as a race against the clock rather than a last step tacked onto the job.

## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in Riverside

We start with an assessment of how far the smoke traveled, not just where the fire burned, because in homes with original balloon framing that's rarely the same footprint. From there we isolate affected zones with negative air containment, HEPA-vacuum loose soot from hard surfaces, and treat soft materials, carpet, upholstery, drapery, that have absorbed residue. Ductwork gets inspected and cleaned when smoke has circulated through the system, since running a contaminated HVAC unit just redistributes odor through the rest of the house. Thermal fogging or hydroxyl treatment handles what's left in the air once surface cleaning is done, and we document everything with photos for the insurance file as we go.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Smoke Damage

Protein residue from a kitchen fire behaves differently than the dry, powdery soot from a wood-frame structure fire, and wildfire smoke that's drifted in from regional burns settles differently still, often as a fine film on exterior surfaces and in attic spaces with ridge vents. We match the cleaning agent and method to the residue type rather than running one generic protocol on every job: dry chemical sponges for dry soot on plaster, solvent-based cleaners for protein residue, and sealing primers on porous wood trim that's absorbed odor and won't fully clean with surface treatment alone.
